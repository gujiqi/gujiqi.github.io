Research

I study how environmental filtering, functional traits, dispersal and evolutionary history shape biodiversity across space and time. My work combines field ecology, global databases and quantitative models, from bryophyte community assembly to historical biogeography and multi-taxon community dynamics.

Trait-based biodiversity and community assembly

I am developing WorldBryDat, a global bryophyte functional-trait database covering 6,123 species and approximately 256 trait fields. My research on Tibetan bryophytes combines field surveys (551 plots, 1,008 species, 256 traits), functional traits, phylogeny and HMSC to examine environmental filtering and community assembly.

Eco-evolutionary modelling and historical biogeography

I am developing HmscEE, a spatially explicit framework that extends joint species distribution modelling to study biodiversity through deep time. It integrates occurrences, traits, phylogenies, fossil evidence, palaeoenvironmental reconstructions, continental movement and dispersal opportunities; its bryophyte case study uses approximately 4.4 million occurrence records across 515 million years.

Global biodiversity dynamics and species distribution modelling

Using LIFEPLAN observations, I examine how urbanization relates to community turnover, recurrence and temporal stability across 3,426 bird species and 462,956 molecular taxa of arthropods and fungi. I also contribute to research on marine fish distributions, species distribution models and cross-validation.
