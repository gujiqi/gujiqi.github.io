Research

Bryophyte Traits and Community Assembly

My PhD research explores how environmental conditions, functional traits and evolutionary history shape bryophyte communities. I am developing WorldBryDat, a global bryophyte trait database currently covering 6,123 species and 264 trait fields, including morphological, reproductive, life-history and habitat-preference traits (31). The database brings together scattered trait information to support broader comparisons of bryophyte ecological strategies.

My research on the Tibetan Plateau combines extensive field surveys with functional traits, phylogenies and environmental data. An ongoing study covering 551 plots and 1,008 species examines how environmental filtering, trait differentiation and evolutionary conservatism contribute to community assembly (28). Related studies explore bryophyte diversity across vegetation types, elevations and host trees, highlighting the roles of both regional environments and local habitat conditions (19, 10).

Eco-evolutionary Modelling and Historical Biogeography

I am developing HmscEE, a spatially explicit framework that extends joint species distribution modelling to study biodiversity across geological time (30). By bringing together species occurrences, functional traits, phylogenies, fossil evidence, palaeoenvironmental reconstructions, continental movements and dispersal opportunities, HmscEE aims to understand how past environments and geographic changes have shaped present-day biodiversity. Its ongoing bryophyte application draws on approximately 4.4 million occurrence records spanning around 515 million years.

Alongside HmscEE, I work with joint species distribution models (JSDMs), particularly the HMSC framework, to study how environmental conditions, species traits and evolutionary relationships shape ecological communities. This includes methodological research and practical guidance on HMSC (24), as well as contributions to JSDM Studio (29), a graphical application for Windows and macOS designed to make joint species distribution modelling more accessible to ecologists.

Global Biodiversity Change

Through the LIFEPLAN project, I study how urbanization influences community turnover and temporal stability across birds, arthropods and fungi (27). The research draws on global biodiversity data comprising 3,426 bird species and 462,956 molecular operational taxonomic units, allowing community dynamics to be compared across different taxonomic groups.

I also collaborate on species distribution modelling, including studies of marine fish distributions, the integration of expert range maps and occurrence records, and model evaluation and cross-validation (26, 25, 22, 20).

Other Research and Collaborations

My other research includes bryophyte taxonomy, species distributions, functional trait variation and plant community ecology (16, 15, 12, 21). Earlier projects examined soil nitrogen dynamics under freeze–thaw cycles and applications of artificial intelligence in environmental monitoring (5, 4).

Across these areas, I aim to connect field observations, global biodiversity data and quantitative models to understand how ecological and evolutionary processes shape biodiversity across space and time.
